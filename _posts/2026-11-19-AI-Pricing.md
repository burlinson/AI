Early in June, Uber announced that its staff had burned through its [entire 2026 AI budget in just four months](https://marketmeglobal.com/ai-news-june-2026-weekly-roundup). The phenomenon was given a name: [“tokenmaxxing”](https://www.buildfastwithai.com/blogs/ai-news-today-june-29-2026), the practice of spending on AI models with no understanding what value was realized.

Uber’s fix was similar to almost every other company: Impose a monthly token cap for each employee, with a manager’s sign-off required to get more.

**Capping employee token use positions AI as a cost to be minimized rather than a tool for optimizing productivity.** It punishes innovation and trains employees to avoid AI. It’s a bit like firms rationing printer paper or email storage. The only people who are happy are the accountants in Finance.

If cost is a legitimate concern, a more nuanced approach is needed: Identify runaway automated jobs, or look closely at teams that are heavy users and understand what they are doing with AI. 

In parallel with the advent of tokenmaxxing, the frontier models started cutting prices.

The Chinese labs started first. In May DeepSeek confirmed that a 75% discount on its model would become permanent. Xiaomi cut prices by up to 99% later that month.

Cuts to the US models followed in late July. OpenAI cut prices by up to 80%. Google followed in August with cuts of up to 50%. Anthropic followed with cuts of up to 90%.

The race to the bottom on pricing is already underway.

**Cheaper tokens won’t end tokenmaxxing; they’ll accelerate it.** 

Every price cut allows companies to consume more tokens and therein allow their employees to explore how AI can help their business grow.

**Smart companies will take the time to understand token value by tying spend to specific business outcomes.** Counting tokens tells accountants what AI costs, whereas valuing business outcomes tells companies if the spend on AI was worthwhile.

The same logic applies to the labs selling the tokens. How will they make money if they are all engaged in a price-cutting war?

It’s a volume game. 

Each token costs less, but usage continues to grow, so revenue keeps climbing. 

The successful AI firms will help both their customers and themselves by making it easy for firms to tie token usage to business outcomes. This is exactly what the successful cloud companies did when cloud computing took off. It wasn't about the storage, it was about the business outcomes. Similarly, for the AI firms, it's not about the tokens, it's about focusing on the business outcomes.  

**If history is any guide, the battle between the AI firms will move from price to volume, then to the deepest and broadest coverage, and finally to ease of use.** 

By then the price may be zero, but there will be a hidden cost for both corporate and personal customers.

Search engines followed the same path in the 1990s. 

AltaVista, Excite and Lycos fought a war over who could index the most of the web. Google won that war by being smarter and simpler: It served up a simple web page that loaded instantly with no advertising. Search became free for everyone, but the hidden cost was our personal information that Google sold to serve up targeted ads. Advertising now brings in about 73% of Alphabet’s revenue.

When the cost of AI tokens hits zero, who is really paying, and what information are we going to be willing to give up for free AI? 

Search engines learned what products we wanted in the material world. 

AI's learning is much more profound: What we are thinking. It will be there to influence us as we reason, decide, choose, and then take physical action in the real-world.

**The social, economic and political implications are enormous: AI will know how to nudge each individual’s internal thought processes, at just the right moment, to prompt action in the physical world.**
